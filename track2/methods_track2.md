# Track 2 — methods description (draft)

## Please describe your approach in detail (how you went from variant/mechanism to candidate medication(s)).

Starting point: the Track 1 diagnosis, MVA1 caused by biallelic loss of function of BUB1B — c.2210T>G p.(Leu737Ter) (null) and c.3006T>G p.(Asn1002Lys) (predicted folding hypomorph). (1) Mechanism: low BUBR1 weakens the spindle-assembly checkpoint and gives two consequences from one defect — chromosome missegregation/mosaic aneuploidy (cancer predisposition) and BUBR1-insufficiency-driven senescence (progeroid features). (2) Druggability gate: BUB1B is pan-essential (DepMap median CRISPR effect −1.21) and has zero drugs or clinical candidates in Open Targets; the only compounds near it are spindle poisons and chemotherapy, which would weaken the checkpoint further. We therefore searched downstream, on the two consequences. (3) Candidate generation and testing: a curated mechanistic analysis plus ten computational or data-driven analyses (C1–C10): network proximity of 8,706 drugs to four disease modules (Guney closest distance, STRING v12, z versus 1,000 degree-matched sets); robustness by personalized PageRank at two STRING thresholds; Europe PMC full-text mining; L1000CDS2 signature reversal with literature signatures and with a primary BubR1-insufficiency signature (GEO GSE277997); Enrichr enrichment of the 187-gene BUB1B interactome; ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 precedent with verified eligibility ages; dosing, interactions and pharmacogenomics (PharmCAT 3.4.0 on the proband's variants); openFDA FAERS paediatric safety; Open Targets/DepMap target genetics. (4) Triage: we kept agents that act downstream, are on the market, and are supported by most lines (convergence matrix, report Fig. 4), then ordered them by clinical evidence in children, kept separate from mechanistic strength. Result: metformin (approved drug; paediatric precedent in Fanconi anaemia, NCT03398824), nicotinamide riboside (dietary supplement; NAD⁺–SIRT2–BUBR1 axis; tolerated in children with ataxia-telangiectasia) and fisetin (dietary supplement; senolytic; adult data only), with tumour surveillance as context and a proposed n-of-1 monitoring frame (GDF15/FGF21 and T-cell p16INK4a as exploratory biomarkers). (5) Verification: a script re-checks every number in the report against its source file (138/138), and every literature claim was checked against its abstract or full text (track2/literature_check.md).

## Was candidate identification automated (e.g., via a drug-target database query or LLM-assisted search), or primarily manual literature review and expert curation?

Both. Candidate generation was largely automated: scripted queries of drug–target databases and APIs, network algorithms, signature reversal and full-text mining over the whole drug space (8,706 drugs in C1). The initial curated hypotheses were drafted with AI assistance and then checked against PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov; the computational analyses then tested whether the same drugs emerge from unbiased searches, and whether anything else does (nothing new, clean and approved did). The final selection and ranking were manual, with explicit rules: acts downstream of the gene, marketed, supported by most lines of evidence, ranked by evidence in children.

## Please describe any manual literature review or expert curation in detail.

(a) Mechanism: primary papers on BubR1-hypomorphic mice (Baker 2004, 2011), aneuploidy-selective compounds (Tang 2011), the NAD⁺–SIRT2–BubR1 axis (North 2014) and the 2024 cancer-surveillance guideline for MVA (Nakano 2024). (b) Clinical precedent: the full registry record of each of 15 trials was read for eligibility ages, doses and outcomes (C7); 5 of 15 include children. (c) Dosing and safety: trial protocols, published results (Pollard 2022 for metformin in Fanconi anaemia; Veenhuis 2021 for NR in ataxia-telangiectasia) and drug labels, combined with the proband's pharmacogenomic calls. (d) Verification: every literature claim in the report was checked against its PubMed abstract or full text; about a dozen statements from working notes were corrected or removed (for example an unsupported cancer-risk figure, trials wrongly described as paediatric, a dose that did not match the protocol). The record is in track2/literature_check.md.

## How did you characterize the variant's mechanism (e.g., loss-of-function vs. gain-of-function, pathway disrupted, downstream biological consequence)?

Biallelic loss of function. Allele A, c.2210T>G p.(Leu737Ter) in exon 17, truncates the protein (null; ClinVar pathogenic). Allele B, c.3006T>G p.(Asn1002Lys) in the C-terminal pseudokinase domain: in the AlphaFold model (pLDDT 91), Asn1002 is buried in the hydrophobic core of the C-lobe (161 heavy atoms within 10 Å of its Cα) and is about 19 Å and 27 Å from the HRD-like (HGD 880–882) and DFG-like (DFS 911–913) motifs, so the change is expected to cause a folding defect (hypomorph), not a catalytic one. This fits the expectation that viable MVA1 needs at least one hypomorphic allele (complete BubR1 loss is embryonic-lethal in mice). Pathway: BUBR1 is part of the mitotic checkpoint complex (BUBR1–BUB3–MAD2–CDC20); low BUBR1 → premature anaphase → missegregation → mosaic aneuploidy → tumour predisposition; and BUBR1 insufficiency → p16-positive senescence → progeroid features (BubR1-hypomorphic mice). We tested this two-axis framing with data rather than assuming it: the BUB1B interactome is enriched for both the checkpoint (adjusted p = 2.5×10⁻¹²¹) and cellular senescence (3.7×10⁻²⁶) (C6), and primary BubR1-insufficient tissue (GSE277997) induces senescence and integrated-stress genes (Cdkn1a, Cdkn2b, Gdf15, Fgf21) (C4).

## Provide a method abstract (up to 500 words). Please include strengths and limitations of your approach as well as methodological detail.

Background. PROBAND01 has mosaic variegated aneuploidy type 1 (MVA1) from biallelic BUB1B loss of function (a null plus a predicted folding hypomorph). Low BUBR1 weakens the spindle-assembly checkpoint, producing two consequences from one defect: chromosome missegregation with cancer predisposition, and premature cellular senescence with progeroid features. No cause-directed therapy exists. Methods. A druggability gate (DepMap, Open Targets) showed that the gene itself is pan-essential and undrugged, so the search moved downstream. A curated mechanistic analysis was cross-checked by ten computational or data-driven analyses: network proximity of 8,706 drugs to four disease modules and its robustness under personalized PageRank; full-text literature mining; signature reversal with literature signatures and with a primary BubR1-insufficiency signature (GEO); pathway enrichment of the BUB1B interactome; clinical precedent with verified eligibility ages; dosing, interactions and pharmacogenomics for this child; paediatric safety reports; and target genetics. Candidates were kept if they act downstream, are marketed and are supported by most lines, and were ranked by clinical evidence in children rather than by mechanistic appeal. Results. The two-axis framing is supported by data that did not assume it: the BUB1B interactome is enriched for both checkpoint and senescence terms, and BubR1-insufficient mouse tissue induces senescence and integrated-stress genes. Three agents emerge: metformin (approved; paediatric precedent in Fanconi anaemia with a tested dose and no metabolic acidosis), nicotinamide riboside (dietary supplement; another NAD⁺ precursor, NMN, raised BubR1 in mice via SIRT2; tolerated in children with ataxia-telangiectasia) and fisetin (dietary supplement; senolytic; adult data only). Tumour surveillance, with renal ultrasound every three months to age seven, remains the recommended backbone. No pharmacogenomic barrier was found; kidney function must be monitored on metformin. We propose a sequential single-patient frame with exploratory biomarkers (serum GDF15/FGF21, T-cell p16INK4a). Strengths. Independent data sources and algorithms; mechanism kept separate from paediatric evidence; every number re-checked by script against its source (138/138) and every literature claim checked against primary text; public data only, on a dual-core laptop, with seed genes in a short configuration block, so the pipeline transfers to other recessive loss-of-function disorders. Limitations. No interventional evidence in MVA1; the computational methods share the two-axis framing, which C6, C4 and the literature test separately; the primary signature rests on one mouse tissue; metformin's aneuploidy selectivity is extrapolated from AICAR cell screens; NR's link to BUBR1 is pre-clinical and fisetin has no paediatric data; NR and fisetin are supplements with variable purity; pharmacogenomic calls are research-grade; the phase of the two alleles is unproven. This is a hypothesis for clinical discussion, not a treatment.
